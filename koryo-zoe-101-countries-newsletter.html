From Liverpool
to Kabul

My road to 101 countries (and counting)
Hi there,
I never had a plan to visit all of the countries in the world. I didn’t even start counting until two years ago, when a fairly traumatic life event led me to pursue a goal so I could focus on something else.
But I was born with an insatiable desire to learn, explore, and talk to absolutely everyone on the planet. And this year, after 18 years of travel, I made it to country no. 101.
I don’t come from a family of travellers, and I’m not writing this because my upbringing was particularly interesting (it really wasn’t). I’m writing it because maybe, just maybe, it’ll show one or two of you that it doesn’t matter where you start. So grab a cup of tea…
 
Not because of some badge of honour, but because I simply cannot give a reason not to visit any specific country.

The Road So Far

How one thing, slowly but surely, led to another

 
Age 15 · France & Monaco
My first time abroad
Growing up in a single-parent family, holidays meant Wales. Then a school friend brought me along on their family holiday to France and I finally got a passport. We even drove into Monaco. Two countries in one go! Spain and Greece followed, and for their generosity I am eternally grateful. I was hooked, and soon I was paying my own way with part-time jobs and realising I could do it all by myself, too.
 
Age 18 · Austria
Au pair in the vineyards
Always having to do something different, I chose Austria over Germany to improve my German. I lived on 300 EUR a month in Burgenland, met Krampus (horrific), and became ‘the British girl you have to speak Hochdeutsch to’. It was the first time my mind was truly opened. Life can be different, and that’s absolutely fine.
 
2015/16 · Japan & North Korea
‘Can you visit North Korea?’
For entirely no reason, I chose Japanese at university in Newcastle. On my year abroad, my Japanese friends kept telling me the UK was too dangerous to visit. I’d just come from there and hardly noticed a thing. So one evening I went home and decided to visit a country I thought was scary because of what I’d heard in the media. The natural choice was North Korea. I googled it, and the rest is history.
 
Beijing · Koryo Tours
‘Hey, I think I’d be kind of good at this job’
That trip was my first ever group tour, and watching our guides planted a seed. When the job came up just as I was graduating, I couldn’t help but seize it. It would buy me some time before I got a ‘real’ job, I thought. Three years in our cosy Beijing office followed, visiting North Korea over 30 times and making real friends there who I still think about a lot to this day.
 
2020 onwards · Tonga, London, Taiwan
So I carried on studying
Then COVID locked me out of China and onto an island in the South Pacific. Armed only with a laptop, I did a master’s on North Korea in the media. Then, realising that ignoring the politics had been naive, a second one in London, working as a nanny again to keep a roof over my head. Then a PhD scholarship in Taiwan, where I still live today. Still studying. Still waiting on North Korea to open.
 
Recent years · Afghanistan & beyond
Second, third, fourth and fifth homes
Waiting on North Korea only led to disappointment year after year, so I channelled that same passion into other misunderstood places. Afghanistan, Iraq, Syria and many more. I’ve spent months in Afghanistan, and I’m proud to have set up our first completely women-run, women-focused tours there, training up and working alongside local female guides. This is what this industry is all about for me.
 

Bigger and smaller, all at once

I’m grateful to have built a life where my friends and loved ones are both everywhere and nowhere at the same time. Hearing my name shouted on the streets of Kabul by someone I’d taken to North Korea for the marathon a few months before. Bumping into friends I hadn’t seen for years in the Babur Gardens. Accidentally staying in the same hostel in Fiji as people I’d met on the road, and sharing a beer.
The more you explore, the more you realise there is to explore. And the more you realise how similar we all are.
After 101?
None of this came without a whole lot of hardship. Hard sleeps on train station bathroom floors, friends lost along the way, and a bank account that has more digits with a ‘-’ in front than not. As friends settle down, I’m left behind in many ways. But so much more fulfilled in others.
I truly believe that if you want something in this world enough, and you make choices focused on this, then it will happen. But it will take great sacrifice.
So, the road to 195 (or however you count them) is the one I’ll slowly carry on walking. As always, in no rush.
If you’d like the long version (Krampus, au pair families from hell and all), I’ve written the whole thing up on the blog. And if you’ve got your own story of how travel found you, hit reply. I’d genuinely love to hear it.
