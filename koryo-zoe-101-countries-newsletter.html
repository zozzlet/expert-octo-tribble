From Liverpool
to Kabul

Our Zoe on 18 years of travel, three degrees, one very long wait for North Korea, and the road to country number 101 (and counting).
Hi there,
If you have ever emailed us, read the blog, or bumped into someone from Koryo in a hotel lobby somewhere unlikely, there is a good chance you have met Zoe. This year she reached her 101st country, and she has written up how a girl from a working-class suburb of Liverpool ended up here.
It is not a story about ticking boxes. Zoe only started counting two years ago. It is about curiosity, languages, a lot of stubbornness, and people. So put the kettle on. Here is the short version.
 
“Not because of some badge of honour, but because I simply cannot give a reason not to visit any specific country.”

The Road So Far

One thing, slowly but surely, led to another

 
Age 15 · France & Monaco
A passport, finally
Family holidays meant Wales. Then a school friend invited Zoe along to France, and they drove into Monaco too. Two countries in one go. Spain and Greece followed, and soon she was paying her own way with part-time jobs and heading off solo.
 
Age 18 · Austria
Au pair in the vineyards
A gap year in Burgenland on 300 EUR a month. Wine under 5 EUR a bottle, a first encounter with Krampus, and the realisation that life does not have to follow one set path.
 
2015/16 · Japan & North Korea
“Can you visit North Korea?”
On a year abroad in Japan, friends kept saying the UK was too dangerous to visit. Zoe had just come from there and noticed nothing. So she decided to go somewhere the media had made her nervous about, typed that question into Google, and joined her first ever group tour. Watching the guides, a small seed was planted.
 
Beijing · Koryo Tours
Once a month, every month
The seed became a job with us in our little Beijing office full of North Korean art. Over three years Zoe visited more than 30 times and made real friends there, the kind she still thinks about today.
 
2020 onwards · Tonga, London, Taiwan
When the world shut, she kept studying
COVID locked Zoe out of China and onto a South Pacific island of about 70,000 people. With just a laptop she wrote a master’s thesis on how Chinese and Western media frame North Korea (distinction). A second master’s in London followed, funded by nannying and a couple of extra jobs. Then a PhD scholarship in Political Economy in Taiwan, where she lives today.
 
Recent years · Afghanistan & beyond
New homes, same passion
With North Korea still closed, Zoe poured the same energy into other misunderstood places: Afghanistan, Iraq, Syria and more. She has spent months in Afghanistan, where she set up our first fully women-run, women-focused tours, training and working alongside local female guides.
 

A bigger, smaller world

One of our favourite bits: hearing her name shouted across a street in Kabul by someone she had taken to the Pyongyang Marathon a few months before. Or bumping into old friends in the Babur Gardens. Or sharing a beer in a Fiji hostel with people she had met on the road years earlier.
As Zoe puts it, the more you travel, the bigger and smaller the world becomes at the same time.
After 101?
Zoe is honest that none of this came easy: nights on train station floors, friends lost along the way, and a bank balance that has spent more time below zero than above it. As friends settle down, she is left behind in some ways, and far more fulfilled in others.
“If you want something in this world enough, and you make choices focused on this, then it will happen. But it will take great sacrifice.”
Next stop? The rest of the 195. Slowly, and as always, in no rush.
