There are eleven countries along the path of totality on 2 August 2027. Most people will choose between two of them, and both will be busy.

This guide is about the third option. ## The eclipse, in one section

**Date:** Monday 2 August 2027.

**What happens:** the Moon passes entirely across the Sun. Totality — the phase where the disc is fully covered, the corona appears, and it is safe to look with the naked eye — runs to **6 minutes 23 seconds** at the point of greatest eclipse in southern Egypt.

**The path:** landfall in southern Spain, then across the Strait of Gibraltar and east along North Africa — Morocco, Algeria, Tunisia, Libya, Egypt — then on over Saudi Arabia, Yemen and Somalia. The shadow is about 253 km wide.

**In Libya:** the shadow reaches the coast at around 09:30 UT. **Benghazi sits essentially on the centreline**, with roughly **6 minutes 9 seconds** of totality. Local time in Libya is UT+2 year-round, so this is a mid-morning eclipse — comfortably high in the sky, no scrambling for a horizon.

**Why it is so long:** the Moon is near perigee and the Earth near aphelion, so the Moon appears large and the Sun small; and the path runs at low latitude near local noon, where the Earth's rotation partly keeps pace with the shadow. We have written the full explanation separately.

**Why it matters:** this is the longest totality visible from land between 1991 and 2114.

## Why eastern Libya rather than Spain or Egypt

A fair question, and worth answering properly rather than defensively.

**Southern Spain** is the easy option: cheap flights, familiar infrastructure, no visa for most Western passports. It also gets around four and a half minutes rather than six, sits at the start of the track where the shadow is moving fastest, and will be extremely crowded. August cloud prospects on the Andalusian coast are decent but not the best on the path.

**Luxor** is close to the point of greatest eclipse and gets the full six minutes and change. It is also the single most obvious place on Earth to watch this eclipse, has been identified as such by everybody, and has been filling up for years. Expect crowds on a scale that will shape the day.

**Morocco and Tunisia** both offer good access and reasonable durations, and are perfectly sensible choices.

**Eastern Libya** offers a combination that nowhere else on the track quite matches:

- The centreline comes ashore here, so you get essentially the maximum duration available anywhere outside Egypt.
- Eclipse-day cloud prospects along this coast are among the best on the entire path — this is desert climate in high summer, and the August record is close to cloudless.
- Almost nobody will be there. Libya receives a tiny number of leisure travellers annually. You will not be standing in a field with ten thousand other people.
- And there is somewhere genuinely extraordinary to spend the days either side — the Greek cities of the Pentapolis, which almost no traveller ever sees.

The trade-off is that Libya requires organising. That is the rest of this guide.

## Visas and permits

**The short version: you cannot travel independently, and we handle the paperwork.**

Libya does not operate a tourist visa you can apply for on your own initiative. Entry for leisure travel is arranged through a licensed local operator who sponsors the visa and secures the associated permissions. There is no visa on arrival for tourism, and no e-visa route.

In practice the sequence looks like this:

1. **You book, and send us your passport scan and details.** We need this early — considerably earlier than for most destinations.
2. **Our Libyan partner files for your visa authorisation** with the relevant authorities. This produces an approval reference.
3. **The approval is issued**, and depending on your nationality and where you are flying from, you either collect the visa at a Libyan consulate or receive it against the approval on arrival. We will tell you which applies to you.
4. **Internal permits** for the archaeological sites and for movement within Cyrenaica are arranged in parallel by our partner. You do not deal with any of this yourself.

**What this means for you practically:**

- **Book early.** This is the single most important line in this guide. Visa processing for Libya is not fast and not entirely predictable, and eclipse-period demand will not help. We ask for passport details months ahead, not weeks.
- **Your passport needs at least six months' validity** beyond the travel date and a couple of clear pages.
- **An Israeli stamp or visa in your passport is a problem.** This remains the case for Libya. If this applies to you, talk to us before booking rather than after.
- **Nationality matters.** Some passports are more straightforward than others. Tell us what you hold at the enquiry stage and we will give you a straight answer about likelihood rather than a hopeful one.
- **Solo female travellers and mixed groups** are both fine on our trips; ask us if you want specifics on how this works in practice.

## Getting there

Eastern Libya is served through **Benghazi (BEN)**, with **Tripoli (TIP)** the alternative in the west of the country.

Realistic routings involve one connection, typically through Istanbul, Cairo, Tunis or a Gulf hub. Flight availability into Benghazi is thinner than into Tripoli, and schedules on these routes change more often than on established leisure routes — which is an argument for booking flights only once your visa position is confirmed, and for using flexible fares if you can.

**We will advise on routing** as part of booking, and we can tell you which connections have been reliable recently rather than which merely exist on paper. International flights are not included in the tour price.

A practical warning worth stating plainly: **do not book a non-refundable flight arriving the day before the eclipse.** Regional aviation has a way of producing delays, and there is no second chance on 2 August. Build in slack.

## How hard is it physically?

Moderate. Not a trekking holiday, not a bus tour either.

**What you will actually be doing:**
- Walking around large archaeological sites, mostly on uneven ground — broken paving, loose stone, worn steps. Cyrene in particular is spread across a hillside and involves slopes.
- Two to three hours on your feet at a stretch, with breaks and shade available but not guaranteed at every point.
- Some road transfers of a couple of hours.
- Standing outside for an extended period on eclipse morning.

**The heat is the real consideration.** This is North Africa in early August. Inland daytime temperatures can be high; the coast and the Green Mountain are meaningfully cooler than the interior, which is one of several reasons the itinerary sits where it does. We start early, break in the middle of the day where the schedule allows, and carry water.

**You should be comfortable** walking a few kilometres over rough ground in warm conditions, and managing your own day bag.

**Talk to us before booking if** you have mobility limitations, a heart or respiratory condition, or difficulty with heat. We would far rather have that conversation early and honestly than have someone struggling at Cyrene in August. Some of it can be worked around; some of it genuinely cannot.

**Medical facilities in eastern Libya are limited.** Bring any prescription medication in sufficient quantity, in original packaging, with a copy of the prescription. Do not plan to buy anything specific locally.

## Money, connectivity and practicalities

**Currency** is the Libyan dinar. **Bring cash in euros** to exchange — Western cards do not work, ATMs are not a realistic option for foreign cards, and you should assume a fully cash economy for anything personal.

**Mobile and internet** are patchy and slower than you are used to. Roaming may or may not work depending on your provider. Hotel wifi exists and is variable. Plan on being substantially offline, and tell people at home to expect that rather than worry.

**Power** is 230V with European two-pin plugs. Outages happen; a power bank is a sensible thing to carry, particularly with a week of photography ahead.

**Alcohol is illegal in Libya.** Not discouraged — illegal. Do not attempt to bring any in.

**Dress** modestly: shoulders and knees covered for everyone, and a scarf useful for women at religious sites. Loose, light, long-sleeved clothing is also simply the sensible response to desert sun.

**What to pack for the eclipse specifically:**
- Certified ISO 12312-2 eclipse glasses — we provide these, but a spare pair of your own is never a bad idea
- A solar filter for any lens you intend to point at the partial phases
- Lens blower and microfibre cloth. **The enemy here is dust, not cloud.**
- Sun hat, high-factor sunscreen, more water than you think
- A head torch, useful more often than you would expect
