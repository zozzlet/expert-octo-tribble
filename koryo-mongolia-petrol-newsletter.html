The story, from Rich
Mongolia based Tour Leader, Koryo Group • Lives in Ulaanbaatar with his family

Fuel vouchers usually do the trick. Rich had stocked up on Petrovis vouchers before heading out on his usual summer routes, standard prep for anyone who drives tours around Mongolia. Then a joke with his guide Chingun about flirting his way to extra fuel did not go quite to plan. The woman at the pump had heard every word, and doubled their fuel anyway, laughing, in perfect English.

Back in Ulaanbaatar, it got serious. Queues stretched to four hours, then six, then eight. Vehicles were rationed by odd and even number plates, and with no seaport of its own and a heavy reliance on Russian fuel, Mongolia did not have an easy backup plan.

Then his daughter asked to come too. Baseball caps on, they joined a queue of a couple hundred cars outside the Bayangol Hotel. Pizza was eaten. Fizzy drinks normally forbidden were, just this once, allowed. Several hours in, on the Peace Bridge, the car gurgled, spluttered, and stopped. They had run out of petrol. While queuing for petrol.

What followed involved a length of rope, several strangers who did not think twice about helping, a round of Coca-Cola bought as a thank you, and one very proud dad watching the numbers finally climb on a petrol pump after more than eight hours. It is exactly the side of Mongolia our guests never have to think about, and honestly, one of our favourite stories of the summer.
