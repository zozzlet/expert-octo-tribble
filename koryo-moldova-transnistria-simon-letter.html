Why Moldova & Transnistria?

•Moldova: Europe’s least-visited country, and a genuinely remarkable one, sitting right now at a crossroads for where it goes next
•Chisinau: a low-key, welcoming, and architecturally surprising capital, equal parts Soviet grit, imperial leftovers, and charm you don’t expect to find
•Cricova: an entire underground wine city, and about as good a way as any to understand how much the grape matters here, to identity as much as to the economy
•Gagauzia: an autonomous region with murky origins and one of the odder ethnic and political set-ups anywhere in Europe
•Transnistria: the Pridnestrovian Moldavian Republic, to give it its full and mostly unrecognised name. A breakaway state full of Soviet iconography, a proper sense of the forbidden, and about as deep an experience of a frontline, complicated de facto country as you’ll get
•Tiraspol: Transnistria’s capital, where Soviet city planning and iconography are taken entirely seriously rather than treated as heritage. Statues and monuments everywhere, plus the KVINT cognac distillery, which is a highlight in itself
•North, South, and Central Transnistria: villages, industrial towns, the genuinely impressive Bender Fortress, this is far more than a one-city trip, we’re covering the length of the place
