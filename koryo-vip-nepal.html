The icons

Every must-see in the valley

The great UNESCO World Heritage Sites of the Kathmandu Valley, lovingly restored since the earthquake — plus a mountain flight that puts Everest outside your window.

  The Everest flight — a one-hour scenic flight along the Himalayas with Mt. Everest filling the window. Window seat guaranteed.
  Pashupatinath & Boudhanath — one of Hinduism's holiest shrines beside the cremation ghats, and one of the world's largest stupas. Intense, sacred, unforgettable.
  The Durbar Squares — Kathmandu (with a rare chance to see the Kumari living goddess), medieval Bhaktapur, and Patan with one of the region's finest museums.
  Swayambhunath — the Monkey Temple's panoramic views over the valley, plus Changu Narayan, a Hindu temple some 3,000 years old.
  The Himalayan hike — an accessible four-hour walk from Dhulikhel to Namobuddha through villages and farmland, with the option to hop in the car at half-hour intervals. Want more trekking? We can build a private add-on itinerary after the tour.

The cultural adventure

Don't just watch — get stuck in

The heart of this tour is hands-on: crafts, kitchens, classrooms and monastery courtyards in the Newar villages where traditional life carries on as it has for centuries.

  A night in a monastery — sleep at Namobuddha, a sacred Buddhist pilgrimage site with breathtaking mountain views, and slip into the rhythm of the monks' day.
  Learn to cook momo — Nepal's famous dumplings, folded and steamed in a traditional cooking class for lunch. Plus a pottery class with Bhaktapur's internationally renowned artisans.
  An afternoon that gives back — volunteer at Shree Chuni Devi Primary School in Bhaisepati, then share a community dinner and camp overnight with your hosts.
  Villages where time stands still — Khokana's mustard-oil presses, Bungmati's woodcarvers, historic Panauti, and a family-run art school where mandala painters work before your eyes.
  Sound bath & Tibetan tea — an hour of meditation with hand-made singing bowls, then locally brewed tea in the Tibetan refugee neighbourhood and its famous carpet workshops.
