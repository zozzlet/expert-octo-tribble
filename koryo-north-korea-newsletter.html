The Big Question

Can I travel to North Korea?

Not yet.
North Korea is not currently open to tourism for the general public. No opening dates set, no criteria announced, and no reliable news yet, despite the rumours. All subject to change the moment the powers-that-be change their minds. Here’s the picture at a glance:

The brief, from Simon Cockerell
General Manager, Koryo Tours • 180+ trips to the DPRK

Tourism closed abruptly in January 2020 and has barely cracked open since. Rason opened for a few weeks in early 2025; Russian passport holders have had (limited) access since February 2024. For everyone else, there’s currently no way in as a tourist.

Other doors exist, but they’re narrow. Delegation visas for special events have got some people in. A trade fair, and the 2025 Pyongyang International Film Festival, where foreign filmmakers and jury members took part (Koryo helped find them). Business visas exist too, but require an authorised, sanctions-clear inviting organisation. Frankly, a non-starter in almost all cases.

The Pyongyang Marathon remains the best bet. Around 200 amateur athletes ran in 2025; 2026 was sadly cancelled; it will happen in 2027, date and details TBC. Koryo Tours is the only official partner of the marathon. Ignore updates not sourced from us.

We’re in constant contact with our partners in Pyongyang and around the country. The moment there’s news, you’ll get it here, on the website, and on our socials first. And we sincerely hope to take you to the world’s most fascinating and complicated country… soon!
