Hello, Timor-Leste!

Asia's youngest country turns 25, and we'll be there for the party. Plus a small plane to an enclave most maps forget, and a road trip into Indonesia's last kingdom.
Hi there,
We've been taking curious travellers to rather unusual places since 1993, and today we get to add a brand-new one to the map: Timor-Leste, also known as East Timor.
Mountains, coral reefs, strong coffee, and one of the most remarkable independence stories of the last hundred years. On 20 May 2002 it became the first new country of the 21st century. On 20 May 2027 it turns twenty-five, so we've built two tours around the celebrations, and the long one goes somewhere very few people ever get to.

Independence Day in Dili

Twenty-five years ago the flag went up for the very first time. This May, we'll be in the crowd when it goes up again.
✓Flag-raising ceremony with FALINTIL resistance veterans standing alongside the national defence forces, plus speeches from the President and Prime Minister
✓The military parade, then a city-wide street party as the formalities wind down
✓Likurai dancing, traditionally performed by women welcoming warriors home, along with tais weavings, food stalls and faces painted with the flag
✓Independence Eve mass the night before, in Tetum and Portuguese, with thousands of Timorese

More Than a Party

Meet the people who built a country

Timor-Leste's story is best told by the people who lived it. Before the fireworks, we spend our days hearing how independence was won, and meeting the women and young people shaping what comes next.
Political History
A Chat with a Resistance Survivor
A 75-minute conversation with a documented resistance survivor, held in the Xanana Gusmão Reading Room, the very room where the independence movement was once coordinated.
 
Occupation & Resistance
Chega! and Santa Cruz
The Chega! exhibition inside the former Balide prison, the Resistance Archive, then we walk the route of the 1991 march from Motael Church to Santa Cruz Cemetery.
Women's Crafts
Weaving the Future
The Alola Foundation champions women's economic empowerment and tais weaving cooperatives. Browse their Alola Esperansa craft shop, then shop direct from weavers at the Tais Market.
 
Social Enterprise
Atauro's Craft Cooperatives
By tuk-tuk to Biojoia de Atauro, where hearing-impaired women make jewellery from coconut shell, seeds and wood, and Boneca de Atauro for handmade embroidered dolls and bags.
Also on the programme: Ba Futuru, a peacebuilding group working with young people through theatre, film and photography; the Dare WWII Memorial in the cool hills above Dili; and a sunrise climb up 570 steps to Cristo Rei.
Both Tours
A Night on Atauro Island
Over 400 coral species, dawn snorkelling, and the odd dugong, dolphin or whale shark passing by. Then a beach BBQ as the sun goes down.

Oecusse: the enclave almost nobody visits

Oecusse is a pocket of Timor-Leste completely surrounded by Indonesia, and one of the least-visited corners of Southeast Asia. There is no easy way in, so we're doing it properly: a private charter flight from Dili, 45 minutes in a small plane over the Timorese coast.
✓Lifau Monument, where the Portuguese first landed on Timor in 1515
✓The Oesilo mud volcanoes, bubbling away in a strange grey landscape near the border
✓The ZEESM ghost buildings, the half-built, overgrown remains of a grand economic zone that never happened
✓Pante Makassar, with its colonial quarter, waterfront sunsets and a beach for swimming

Long Tour • Crossing Into Indonesia

Overland to West Timor and the last kingdom of Timor

Same island, two countries. From Oecusse we cross the land border at Wini into Indonesian West Timor, then head up into the highlands of South Central Timor, the last stretch by 4WD.
 
A night in Boti Village
Boti is often called the last kingdom of Timor. Its king and his people still follow Halaika, the ancestral belief most of the island left behind. We bring a traditional gift for the king, sleep in the village homestay, and in the morning walk among the thatched ume kbubu houses and watch weavers colour cloth with plant dyes.
Down to Kupang
On the way, a dip at Camplong's spring pool and a stop at Oebelo, where families make and play the sasando, a palm-leaf string instrument. In Kupang: the glowing Crystal Cave, the 1653 Dutch Fort Concordia, grilled seafood and smoky se'i at the night market, and a farewell dinner at Cafe Inklusi, run entirely by Kupang's deaf community.
